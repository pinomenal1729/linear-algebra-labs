The Random Surfer

Imagine a person who repeatedly: (1) visits a webpage, (2) randomly clicks one of its outgoing links, (3) keeps browsing… forever. Where will they spend most of their time?

Proportions of time

Watch the proportions stabilize. That stable mix of time is what PageRank will turn out to be.
Notice: the surfer never “thinks” about importance. Importance emerges from which pages keep receiving visits. Now let’s write this network down as mathematics.

Transition matrix P

Column j of P describes where probability flows FROM page j — so P·r moves rank forward:
rk+1 = P · rk

🤔 Prediction question
If rk+1 = rk — the ranking stops changing — what does that tell you about the transformation?
The ranking stopped changing. That means P·r = r. The matrix P left the vector r pointing the same way. A vector that a matrix only stretches or shrinks — never turns — is called an eigenvector.

You just discovered an eigenvector

The stable ranking vector satisfies P·r = r = 1·r. A vector that a matrix transforms only by scaling (not turning) is an eigenvector; the scaling factor is the eigenvalue λ.

Power iteration

Your repeated multiplication rₖ = Pᵏ·r₀ is the classic power iteration algorithm: multiply again and again, and the dominant long-term structure (the eigenvector for λ=1) reveals itself.

Dynamical system

xk+1 = P·xk

Current state → matrix → next state → matrix → … This is a dynamical system: how a state changes over time. You will meet this again in “PCA & dynamical systems”.

The fix — damping (teleportation)

With probability α follow a link; with probability 1−α jump to a random page. That makes the Google matrix:

G = α·P + (1−α)·(1/n)·J,  J = all-ones matrix
