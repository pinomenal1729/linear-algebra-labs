How can a machine understand taste?

A platform does not directly know what “interesting”, “comforting” or “intense” means to you. It needs numbers that represent both you and every available title.

The mathematical idea

User preference vector: p ∈ ℝ¹⁰
Movie/show vector: xⱼ ∈ ℝ¹⁰

Similarity = cos(p,xⱼ) = (p · xⱼ) / (‖p‖ ‖xⱼ‖)
Interpretation: two vectors pointing in nearly the same direction represent a strong match—even when their lengths differ.

Turn your ratings into a preference vector

First we subtract your personal mean rating. Then every centred rating becomes a signed weight on the feature vector of that title.

Step A — centre the ratings

TITLErᵢrᵢ − μROLE

Step B — weighted feature average

pᵣ = Σᵢ (rᵢ − μ)xᵢ / Σᵢ |rᵢ − μ|

Final profile: p = 0.68pᵣ + 0.32pₑ

pᵣ comes from your ratings. pₑ comes from the explicit sliders. The blend prevents a few ratings from completely overriding what you told the engine.

4

Measure content similarity

For every unseen item j, the engine computes the cosine of the angle between your profile vector p and the item vector xⱼ.

Cosine similarity

content(j) = 100 × max(0, (p · xⱼ)/(‖p‖‖xⱼ‖))
Why cosine? It compares direction rather than raw magnitude. A title can be a strong thematic match even if its feature values are not numerically large.

5

Add a collaborative signal

The system compares your centred rating pattern with four synthetic viewers. Similar viewers receive more influence over the collaborative score.

Neighbour similarity

sim(u,n) = (ũ · ñ)/(‖ũ‖‖ñ‖)

CF(j) = Σₙ max(0,sim(u,n)) · ratingₙⱼ / Σₙ max(0,sim(u,n))

Why only 24% collaborative?

This small classroom dataset has only four neighbours. Content similarity is therefore the dominant signal, while collaborative filtering acts as a useful correction.

base(j) = 0.76 content(j) + 0.24 CF(j)

Diversity-aware ranking

After selecting one recommendation, the engine penalizes candidates that are too similar to titles already chosen.

adjusted(j) = base(j) − λ · maxₖ cos(xⱼ,xₖ)

λ = 35 × diversity setting

The final scoring rule

base(j) = 0.76 content(j) + 0.24 CF(j)

rank score = base(j) − diversity penalty

Important limitation

This lab uses a small hand-built feature dataset and synthetic neighbours. Real platforms use millions of interactions, implicit signals, embeddings, matrix factorization, deep models and continuous online testing. The mathematical logic, however, begins with the same representations and similarity ideas.
