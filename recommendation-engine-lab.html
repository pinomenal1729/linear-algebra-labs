Step 7 of 7

What you just discovered

A real recommendation engine is more complex, but its core logic begins with the same linear-algebra language you used here.

What you discovered

  • A user’s taste can be represented as a vector.
  • Each title can be represented as a feature vector.
  • Stacking title vectors creates a matrix.
  • Dot product and cosine similarity measure different aspects of a match.

Why this matters

  • Netflix, YouTube, Spotify, and shopping platforms
  • Personalised feeds and search results
  • Recommender systems in AI products
Quick check

Test your understanding

What does the feature matrix store?

Rows represent titles and columns represent content features.

Why use cosine similarity?

It measures whether the pattern of preferences points in the same direction, independent of overall magnitude.

Is the largest raw dot product always the fairest recommendation?

No. Large vector magnitudes can inflate the raw score, so direction-based similarity is also useful.

Common mistakes

  • Thinking recommendations are random.
  • Confusing a user vector with a single rating.
  • Assuming dot product and cosine similarity measure exactly the same thing.
  • Forgetting that feature design affects the recommendations.

Reflection
