Your phone is lost somewhere in the city.

A signal tower can estimate how far away the phone is—but distance alone does not reveal a direction. How many towers must cooperate before the phone has nowhere left to hide?

A location problem became a matrix problem.

Subtracting one circle equation from the others cancels x² and y². What looked nonlinear becomes a linear system.

Quick check

Why is one tower not enough?

It provides a distance but no direction, so every point on a circle remains possible.

What does the third independent tower contribute?

It removes the two-location ambiguity and checks consistency.

Why do real circles fail to meet perfectly?

Signal timing and distance estimates contain measurement error.
