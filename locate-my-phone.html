The exact-location system

The noisy-location problem

When the rings do not meet exactly, the best estimate minimizes the total squared mismatch.

min Σ (measured distance − predicted distance)²

linearized form: AᵀA x̂ = Aᵀb

This is the least-squares idea you used in the final challenge.

Why did Tower A produce a ring?

Distance says how far, but not which direction. Every point on the ring was equally far from A.

Why were A and B still insufficient?

Their two rings intersected at two positions, so both measurements admitted two candidates.

What made Tower C useful?

It supplied independent information: its distance to the two candidates was different, so it could reject one.

Accuracy note: This mission models terrestrial network trilateration. Satellite GPS uses the same distance-from-known-points idea in 3D and generally needs at least four satellites because receiver clock error is also unknown.
