Why Linear Algebra
Had to Be Invented

This page is not a lecture note. It is your doorway into the subject. Before formal definitions, you should feel the need for a mathematical language powerful enough to describe change, structure, direction, relationships, and prediction.

We will not begin by telling you what linear algebra is. We will begin by making you feel why it had to be invented.

The big idea

Linear algebra was invented because we needed a systematic way to talk about many quantities changing together. A single number is often not enough. We need lists of numbers, patterns of relationships, and machines that act on those lists. That is where vectors, matrices, and transformations come from.

What you will meet

  • Vectors — ways to package quantities together.
  • Matrices — machines and relationship tables.
  • Transformations — how points, images, or states change.
  • Eigenvectors — directions that reveal stable structure.
  • Gradients — how to improve by following slope.
